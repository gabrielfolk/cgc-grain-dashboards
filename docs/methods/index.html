Sources and coverage

CGC Grain Statistics Weekly

Canadian Grain Commission, Grain Statistics Weekly, crop years 2013-14 onward. Published on Thursdays for the week ending the Sunday before. The crop year runs Aug 1 to Jul 31: week 1 starts Aug 1 and week 52 ends Jul 31.

Each page's Inputs line gives the week it uses.

Western Canadian grain

Figures cover Western Canadian grain. Wheat and corn graded Canada Eastern (CE grades, grown in Ontario and Quebec and handled at eastern terminals) are left out of exports and terminal stocks, so totals are lower than CGC's all-Canada figures. The weekly flash on crop pages starts from the all-Canada total and splits it into Western Canadian (from port terminals, by port, and direct from primary elevators) and Eastern Canadian. CGC's Terminal Exports total is western terminal exports plus Eastern Canadian; direct exports come from its Primary Shipment Distribution table.

Soybean grades, and corn grades other than CE, do not record origin, so soybean and corn exports and stocks at eastern terminals include grain grown outside Western Canada.

Units

Totals (crop year to date, full-year, stocks, production) are in million tonnes (Mt) on the overview, the big-crop pages and Feed Grains, and in thousand tonnes (kt) on the six special-crop pages (flaxseed, rye, beans, canaryseed, chickpeas, mustard). Weekly figures and rates are always kt (kt a week). Copyable tables are in kt and say so in their first header. Chart axes give their unit on the top tick; margins are C$ per acre ($/ac) and prices C$ per tonne ($/t).

Crop size (production)

StatCan's production estimates for Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta and British Columbia (table 32-10-0359), the region CGC's weekly data covers. Wheat excludes durum. The current crop year uses StatCan's latest in-season estimate, which will be revised, so anything sized to the crop moves when StatCan revises it, not only when farmers deliver.

Weekly values and comparisons

Weekly values

week = crop-year total this week − crop-year total last week

CGC applies revisions only to its crop-year totals, never to its weekly rows, so the weekly rows add up short of the crop-year total, and a revision can make a week lumpy or negative.

revision gap = 1 − sum of CGC weekly rows to week w ÷ CGC crop-year total at week w

Weeks 21 and 22 are one combined holiday report. The 4-week average is the better guide to pace.

LY, 5-yr average and range

LY is the previous crop year. The 5-yr average and range use the five crop years before this one, at the same week of the crop year.

vs 5-yr = this year ÷ 5-yr average at the same week − 1

Pace math

pace needed = (target total − crop year to date) ÷ weeks left

Targets are last year's full-year total and the 5-year average total, compared with the current 4-week average.

Farmer selling

Producer deliveries

What farmers deliver into the licensed system: licensed primary (country) elevators, straight to processors, and producer cars. Grain fed on the farm, or sold farm-to-farm or to feedlots, is not tracked by CGC, so deliveries run below production.

Province tables and charts cover elevator and producer-car deliveries only, because CGC rarely gives a province for deliveries straight to processors.

Share of the crop delivered (sized to the crop)

share delivered = deliveries since Aug 1 ÷ western production of this year's harvest

normal share = average share at the same week over the five previous crop years
vs normal = share delivered ÷ normal share − 1

The range is the lowest and highest share in those five years. It separates selling pace from crop size: deliveries can be up only because the crop is bigger. Needs at least three previous years with a production estimate. Production is the crop size above.

Full-year delivery pace projection

projection = deliveries since Aug 1 ÷ usual share of the year's deliveries in by this week

The usual share is the average over the five previous crop years of (deliveries by this week ÷ that year's full-year deliveries). It assumes a normal seasonal pattern from here on: simple arithmetic, not a forecast. Left blank while the usual share is under 2%.

Pace rank

Deliveries at this week ranked against the same week of every crop year since 2013-14.

Direct to processors

direct share = deliveries straight to processors ÷ all producer deliveries

Country elevators

Deliveries and shipments

CGC's primary elevator figures: grain delivered into licensed country elevators, and shipped out to every destination (port terminals, processors, domestic users and containers).

shipped per tonne delivered = shipments since Aug 1 ÷ deliveries since Aug 1

Net build

net build = deliveries into country elevators − shipments out, since Aug 1

Rising means elevators are filling. Grade transfers and shrink mean it will not match the change in stocks exactly.

Weeks of cover

weeks of cover = stocks ÷ 4-week average of weekly outflow

Country elevators: country elevator stocks over shipments out. Port terminals: terminal stocks at all ports over terminal exports. How long stocks would last at the recent rate. Weeks when the 4-week outflow is under a quarter of that crop year's average week (holidays, freeze-up) are left blank, because the ratio would spike.

Port terminals and exports

Exports

Shipments from licensed port terminals, plus shipments from country elevators that go straight to export or to container loaders (direct exports). Before 2018-19 CGC reports Vancouver and Prince Rupert together as Pacific.

Terminal receipts and the Pacific gap

Receipts are unloads at licensed port terminals, mostly by rail.

Pacific gap = Pacific receipts − Pacific exports, since Aug 1

It does not reconcile exactly with Pacific terminal stocks (the gap drifts by up to about 5% of a year's exports), so read it against its own 5-year range rather than as a stock level. Thunder Bay and St. Lawrence are left out because grain moved on from Thunder Bay is received again downstream.

Thunder Bay shipments

CGC's terminal disposition figures for Thunder Bay: grain sent by lake to other terminals, to Canadian domestic users, or straight to export.

The usual last shipping week is the median, over the last five crop years, of the week before all-grain Thunder Bay shipments drop under 10% of their average.

Exports by destination

UN Comtrade's annual customs records by partner country, calendar years. They cover all of Canada's exports, including grain shipped straight from processors by truck and rail, so they run well above CGC's export figures for crops like mustard. US-reported imports from Canada are shown as a cross-check.

US share = exports to the US ÷ exports to the world

The trend is a straight line through the years shown; the typical swing is the standard deviation of the years around it. Neither is a backtested forecast.

Export grades

CGC's terminal export grades, Western grain only. CGC spells grades differently across years, so they are matched on letters and digits; grades it now reports only as OTHER are grouped in Other grades for every year.

Processing, feed and stocks

Processing

CGC's "Milled/Mfg Grain": grain processed by licensed processors (crush for oilseeds, licensed milling for cereals). Each crop page says what it covers.

Domestic feed shipments

CGC's domestic feed grain table: shipments of feed grain from primary elevators. A subset of the all-grain figures, so feed grain that farmers deliver to elevators is already in producer deliveries. CGC notes that the licensed system handles only about 10–15% of feed grain use; most is fed on farms or sold farm-to-farm and farm-to-feedlot. Our estimate of all feed use is on Feed Grains.

Commercial stocks

Week-end stocks in licensed country elevators, processors and port terminals. Grain stored on farms is not included. Ending stocks are the Jul 31 figure.

stocks cover = commercial stocks ÷ 4-week average of the crop's uses

Uses are exports plus, where the crop page shows them, processing and domestic feed shipments.

Things to watch

Things to watch

Generated from the data at each refresh; nothing is written by hand for a particular week. Crop pages compare each measure with its 5-year average at the same week and show the ones furthest from normal. Early in the crop year, small volumes can make changes look large.

The overview's cross-crop list reads each crop page's data and lists the crops furthest from their own 5-year average at the same week: country elevator shipments per tonne delivered, exports, commercial stocks, weeks of cover at port and Thunder Bay shipments. Small crops are left out of each list, and soybean and corn exports are tagged because they include eastern-grown grain.

Forecasts and models

Canola crush and export forecast

The page leads with the next four weeks, the part with a real edge: each panel compares the model's backtest error 1–4 weeks ahead with the best simple rule (the last 4 weeks' average, that average seasonally adjusted, or the same week last year). The full-year forecast is listed with the simple estimates it is built from, ranked by their backtest error from the current week, so a simple estimate that has done better at this point in the year shows above it.

The weekly model is a ridge regression on recent pace, seasonality, stocks and deliveries; the export model also uses StatCan's latest production estimate. The full-year forecast is a weighted mix of simple estimates, with weights learned from earlier crop years for each week of the year. The 80% ranges come from backtest errors. Soybean-complex price inputs were tested and did not improve accuracy, so they are not used. Method and backtest: reports/canola_forecast.md.

Feed use estimate

A demand-side model: animal numbers and slaughter set the grain energy needed, price and availability split it across grains, and corn is split by origin. How it is built is on Feed Grains; method and limits in reports/feed_estimate.md.

Producer margins

margin = farm price × trend yield − variable costs (per acre)

Saskatchewan farm prices, five-harvest trend yields and Crop Planning Guide budgets. Details on Producer Margins.
