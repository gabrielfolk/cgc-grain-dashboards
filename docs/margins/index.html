About the data

  • Deliveries: Canadian Grain Commission, Grain Statistics Weekly, crop years 2013-14 onward. Producer deliveries are the sum of deliveries into licensed primary elevators, direct deliveries to processors, and producer-car shipments; grain fed on farms or sold outside licensed channels is not included. All 15 crops, with exports and stocks: Overview.
  • Weekly values are the week-over-week change in CGC's crop-year-to-date totals. CGC applies revisions only to those totals, so its own weekly rows add up 1–4% short. A revision can make a week negative.
  • The 5-year average and range use the five crop years before this one, compared week for week. Crop sizes are StatCan's production estimates for Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta and British Columbia (table 32-10-0359). The current year uses StatCan's latest in-season estimate, which will be revised. Wheat excludes durum.
  • Margins: revenue is the Saskatchewan monthly farm price (StatCan table 32-10-0077, published about eight weeks after the month) times a trend yield, the average Saskatchewan yield of the five previous harvests (table 32-10-0359). Variable costs are the Saskatchewan Crop Planning Guide budgets for the Dark Brown soil zone (stubble seeded), published each January: seed, fertilizer, chemicals, fuel, repairs, custom work, insurance and operating interest. The parsed budgets for every zone are in reference/sk_crop_planning_guide.csv. The series starts in 2017, when the guide moved to a higher-input system; earlier budgets are not comparable. These are budget margins for a typical grower, not farm survey results.
  • Margins cover Saskatchewan; deliveries cover Western Canada. Peas use the yellow pea budget and lentils the red lentil budget, against StatCan's all-class prices. Malt and feed barley have separate prices and budgets but share one delivery series. Soybeans are left out (StatCan's Saskatchewan soybean price has too many gaps), as are crops without a budget.
  • Over 2017–2025, harvest-time margins were only weakly related to early-season delivery pace (correlation about 0.2), so the incentive-vs-deliveries chart describes where crops sit now rather than predicting deliveries.
  • The full-year projection scales deliveries so far by the share of the year's deliveries that usually arrive by this week (5-year average). It is a simple pace estimate, not a forecast.
